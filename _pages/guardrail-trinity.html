The question

A good safety score.
Whose policy?

A model can recognize harmful content and still struggle to follow an operator’s rules.

Standard moderation, policy adaptation, and precision on benign inputs test different things. A fixed taxonomy can become a shortcut. Sensitive words can trigger false alarms. Adding reasoning introduces another training objective to balance.

GuardrailTrinity brings these demands into a single data-curation recipe, evaluated across Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen3 at 8B, 14B, and 32B, with three training seeds.

The method

Three changes to the data.
Two ways to decide.

The contribution is how established ingredients work together—and how their behavior changes with recipe, scale, and inference mode.

01 / POLICY

Procedural
safety schemas

Randomize category identifiers, order, and rendering. Train the model to cite the policy in front of it, even when the codes and format change.

List → JSON → ParagraphVary the presentation. Preserve the rule.
02 / CONTEXT

Reasoning chain
decomposition

Describe user intent, agent behavior, and policy match before the verdict. Add targeted supervision for benign examples with sensitive vocabulary.

Intent → Behavior → Policy → VerdictSeparate the request from the response.
03 / MODE

Hybrid
data blend

Mix direct classification and reasoning targets at a nominal 3:1 instance ratio. Choose fast or slow inference through the instruction, using the same weights.

System-1 ↔ System-2One checkpoint. Optional analysis.

Built from AEGIS and WildGuardMix. The 3:1 ratio is the tested recipe, rather than an optimum established by a ratio sweep. Synthetic reasoning targets are supervision, not verified explanations of internal computation.

The evidence

Policy adaptation shows up
when you test for it.

Four base models. Three seeds. Fifteen open-weight baseline configurations. Complementary tests of moderation, policy adherence, and calibration.

Finding 01

Standard moderation scores hide a compliance gap.

On BeaverTails-PolicyShift, Qwen3-32B Hybrid-PN-1 reaches 71.87 ± 0.86% Macro-F1, compared with 64.84% for DynaGuard-Fast, the strongest evaluated baseline on this probe.

All 27 procedural Qwen3 variant means across 8B, 14B, and 32B exceed that baseline. The advantage is already present at 8B.

Scope: PolicyShift remaps and removes categories using a held-out six-category taxonomy. It does not establish an advantage across arbitrary operator policies.

Finding 02

Procedural schemas help across the tested Qwen3 scales.

Procedural variants consistently outperform static-schema variants on PolicyShift at 8B, 14B, and 32B. In the closer Adapt versus Reason comparison, changing the schema gains 6.06, 5.98, and 3.84 pp, respectively.

The plotted groups also differ in training mode and composition. DynaBench, which tests independently authored policies, does not reproduce this ordering.

Finding 03

The fast path retains
custom-policy compliance.

The trade-off

Fewer false alarms can
mean more missed harms.

At Qwen3-32B in fast mode, adding hard negatives to permission-augmented Hybrid reduces both the DynaHate false-positive rate and harmful-input recall.

The practical takeaway

Choose the recipe.
Check the operating point.

Select data composition, scale, and inference mode together. Validate all three on the policy and inputs you intend to use.

01

Test the policy you actually need.

Pair standard moderation scores with custom-policy evaluation. PolicyShift and DynaBench produce different rankings.

02

Measure both sides of each error.

Track missed harms alongside false alarms. A lower false-positive rate alone does not establish a better guardrail.

03

Check seeds and audit the reasoning.

Some 8B recipes are unstable under the tested settings, and 32B slow-mode results can vary substantially. Synthetic chains also contain documented semantic errors.

Scope and limitations +

The experiments use English prompt interfaces and three seeds per configuration. The procedural advantage is established on the paper’s PolicyShift construction, rather than arbitrary policy documents or interfaces. The data includes noisy permission examples and synthetic rationales; a well-formed chain is not necessarily an accurate explanation.

Comparisons across scales also differ in optimization settings. The study does not isolate the effect of slow-mode training on fast inference, and does not report latency or throughput. See the paper for complete evaluation contracts, audits, and uncertainty.
